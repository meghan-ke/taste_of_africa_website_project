Food notes & new traditions

Culture

The quiet revival of West Africa's ancient grains

Fonio is one of the oldest cultivated grains in West Africa. It is tiny, cooks in just a few minutes and has a light, nutty taste. For generations it was eaten mostly in rural homes across the Sahel.

Today chefs are putting it on contemporary menus, in salads, porridges and pilafs. Growers and cooks say the aim is to bring it to new tables while keeping the traditions that protected it.

People

Meet the women preserving Ethiopia's coffee ceremony

In Ethiopia, coffee is roasted, ground and brewed in front of guests, usually by a woman of the household. The coffee is served in three rounds, and incense is often burned during the ceremony.

It is a ritual of hospitality, and it is being carried forward by new generations, including Ethiopians living abroad who recreate it in their own homes.

Ingredients

Why preserved lemon changes everything

Preserved lemon is made by packing lemons in salt and their own juice for several weeks. The rind softens and turns fragrant, and it is the key flavor in many Moroccan tagines.

A little goes a long way: salty, floral and slightly bitter all at once. You can taste it in the Preserved Lemon Tagine.
