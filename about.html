About Tastes of Africa

A home for the recipes, regions and stories behind African food.

Our mission

To celebrate and promote African food heritage by sharing its dishes, its ingredients and the people who keep these traditions alive.

Too often African cooking is described as one cuisine. In reality, each region has its own staples, techniques and flavors. We want every visitor to cook a dish, learn where it comes from and understand why it matters to the people who make it.

A short history of African cuisine

African cooking began with indigenous crops: sorghum, millet, yam, teff and fonio. Over centuries, trade routes brought new ingredients and ideas. Trans-Saharan caravans carried spices and dried fruit north and south, and Indian Ocean trade brought rice, spices and cooking techniques to the east coast.

After the 16th century, crops from the Americas, including maize, cassava, tomatoes and chili peppers, spread across Africa and became central to many dishes. African cooks made these ingredients their own.

Global influence

African food has also shaped kitchens far from the continent. Enslaved Africans carried farming knowledge and ingredients such as okra and rice-growing skills across the Atlantic, and that influence is visible today in Southern US cooking, Caribbean food and Brazilian cuisine. More recently, African chefs and restaurants around the world are bringing their own cuisines to new audiences.

A food landmark: Jemaa el-Fnaa

In Marrakech, Morocco, this famous square fills each evening with food stalls serving grilled meats, snail soup, tagines and fresh juices. It is one of Africa's best-known gathering places for street food.
