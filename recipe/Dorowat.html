East Africa · Ethiopia

Doro wat

2 hr · Serves 6

About the dish

Doro wat is often called Ethiopia's national dish. It is a rich chicken stew built on a huge amount of slowly cooked onions, berbere spice and niter kibbeh (spiced butter), with whole boiled eggs added at the end. It is served at celebrations such as Ethiopian New Year, Christmas and Easter, and the long cooking is part of the occasion. Everyone eats from one shared platter, scooping the stew with pieces of injera.

Ingredients

Doro wat

  • 1.2 kg chicken pieces (drumsticks and thighs), skin removed
  • Juice of 1 lemon, plus 1 tsp salt
  • 6 large red onions (about 1 kg), very finely chopped
  • 1/3 cup niter kibbeh (spiced butter), or butter
  • 3 to 4 tbsp berbere spice blend
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste (optional)
  • 2 cups chicken stock or water
  • 6 hard-boiled eggs, peeled
  • Salt to taste
  • Injera, to serve

Instructions

1. Prepare the chicken

Toss the chicken with the lemon juice and 1 tsp salt and leave for 30 minutes. Rinse and pat dry. Score each hard-boiled egg lightly with a knife so the sauce can soak in.

2. Cook down the onions

Put the chopped onions in a large heavy pot with no oil. Cook on medium-low heat for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring often, until they are soft and reduced to a thick paste. Add a splash of water if they begin to stick.

3. Build the sauce

Add the niter kibbeh, garlic and ginger and cook for 3 minutes. Stir in the berbere and tomato paste and cook on low heat for 10 minutes, until the sauce turns a deep red and the oil rises to the top.

4. Simmer the chicken

Add the chicken and the stock and stir to coat. Cover and simmer on low heat for 45 to 50 minutes, stirring now and then, until the chicken is very tender and the sauce is thick. Season with salt.

5. Add the eggs and serve

Add the eggs and cook for 10 more minutes so they take on the color of the sauce. Spoon the stew over injera and serve with extra injera for scooping.
