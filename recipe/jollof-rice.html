Smoky Jollof Rice

1 hr 15 min · Serves 6

About the dish

Jollof is the dish that welcomes you to almost every west African celebration, from weddings to birthdays. Its name is linked to the Wolof people of Senegambia, and today Nigeria and Ghana each claim their version is the best. A good pot has a deep red color and slightly smoky bottom layer, which cooks call the party rice crust.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice, rinsed
  • 4 large ripe tomatoes (or one 400 g can)
  • 3 red bell peppers
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper, adjust to taste
  • 2 onions, one blended and one sliced
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil
  • 3 tbsp tomate paste
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

1. Make the pepper base

Blend the tomatoes, bell peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion until smooth. Pour into a pot and cook on medium heat for about 20 minutes, until it has reduced and the oil starts to separate.

2. Build the flavor

In a large heavy pot, heat the oil and fry the sliced onion until golden. Add the tomato paste and cook for 3 minutes. Stir in the curry powder, thyme and bay leaves, then add the reduced pepper base and cook for 10 more minutes.

3. Cook the rice

Add the stock and season with salt. Stir in the rice so it is coated in the souce. Cover the pot with foil, then the lid, to trap the steam. Cook on the lowest heat for 30 minutes without stirring.

4. Rest and serve

Turn off the heat and let the pot rest, covered, for 10 minutes. Fluff with a fork, scraping up the crust from the bottom, and serve hot.
