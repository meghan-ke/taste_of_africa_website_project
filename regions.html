Regions

Climate, trade and history shape what each region cooks. Pick a region to see its staples and techniques, then open a dish for the full recipe.

West Africa

Cooking is built on a base of tomato, onion and peppers, with heat from scotch bonnet and richness from palm oil. Meals are often one-pot dishes or stews with a sratch for scooping.

Staples

  • Rice, yam, cassava and plantain
  • Black-eyed peas, groundnuts and fonio
  • Palm oil, scotch bonnet and ginger

Cooking techniques

  • One-pot simmering of rice or stews in a tomato-pepper base
  • Slowly frying a blended pepper, tomato and onion base until the oil separates
  • Grilling meat and fish over an open flame
  • Pounding or stirring yam and cassava into a soft dough to eat with soup

See these techniques in action in Smoky Jollof Rice, a one-pot dish built on the tomato-pepper base.

North Africa

The food of the Mediterranean coast and the Sahara meets in slow-cooked stews, steamed couscous and fragrant spice blends. Sweet, salty and sour flavors often appear in the same dish.

Staples

  • Couscous, wheat, and chickpeas
  • Olives, dates and preserved lemon
  • Saffron, cumin and ras el hanout

Cooking techniques

  • Slow braising meat with spices in a clay tagine
  • Steaming couscous over a simmering stew
  • Preserving lemons in salt for weeks
  • Balancing sweet and savory flavors with dried fruit, olives and spice

See slow braising and preserved lemon in action in Preserved Lemon Tagine.

East Africa

Meals are shared from a large platter, eaten by hand. Ethiopian cooking in particular centers on teff flatbread and richly spiced stews called wot.

Staples

  • Teff, sorghum and maize
  • Lentils, beans and chickpeas
  • Berbere spice and nitter kibbeh (spided butter)

Cooking techniques

  • Fermenting teff batter for several days before cooking flatbread
  • Cooking onions slowly into a deep stew base
  • Simmering lentils, beans or meat in berbere-spiced stews
  • Serving everything on one shared platter and eating by hand

See fermented teff and slow-simmered stews in action in Injera & Misir Wot.

Southern Africa

Cooking over open fire, know as the braai, is a social tradition. Indian and Malay communities added curry and spice traditions that now sit beside maize porridge and grilled meat.

Staples

  • Maize porridge, beef and game
  • Chakalaka relish and peri-peri
  • Cape Malay curry spices

Cooking techniques

  • Braai: grilling meat over wood or charcoal
  • Slow-cooking stews in a cast-iron pot, called a potjie, over the fire
  • Simmering curries with Indian and Cape Malay spice blends

See the Durban curry tradition in action in Durban Bunny Chow, a spiced curry simmered until thick and served in a loaf of bread.
